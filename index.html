How to read this

Why every match says “provisional”

A coverage map is only as good as its evidence. The strongest kind of evidence is the publisher's own standards-to-lesson correlation table, or failing that, the printed “I can” learning targets on each lesson-opener spread. Neither was available when this was built. What was available: lesson titles and the module structure of the 2026-27 district pacing guide.

Titles mislead in a specific way. A module called “Quadratic Functions” almost certainly teaches the discriminant, but whether it teaches “sketch the graph given a verbal description” — the actual wording of A2.QFE.5 — is not something a title can tell you. So each row here is a claim you can accept, downgrade, or reject once you check the book.

What does not depend on titles

Some findings are structural, and those are solid. If no module in the year covers sequences, then no lesson teaches arithmetic or geometric sequences, and no reading of any lesson interior will change that. Those findings are flagged on the Overview tab and marked in red on the Standard first tab. They are the ones worth acting on today.

The trigonometry column

Arkansas 2023 gives Algebra II no trigonometry standards at all — the seven domains are radicals and complex numbers, functions, linear, quadratic, exponential and logarithmic, polynomial and rational, and statistics. Your guide gives Modules 11 and 12 roughly sixteen lessons of trigonometry. That is not an error; it is a defensible choice for ACT and Pre-Calculus readiness. It is worth seeing, though, because it is time that cannot also be spent on the standards that currently have no lesson.
